METHODS & EVIDENCE

An estimate you
can examine.

Field Shift replays historical weather through physical water-balance equations. You can inspect the records, assumptions and validation behind the comparison.

01 · WEATHER

Reference demand

FAO-56 Penman–Monteith converts daily weather into reference evapotranspiration (ET₀): the atmospheric demand for water from a standard reference surface.

ET₀ = [0.408Δ(Rn − G) + γ·900/(T + 273)·u₂(es − ea)] / [Δ + γ(1 + 0.34u₂)]

NASA POWER provides rainfall, temperature, dew point, humidity, wind at 2 m, solar radiation and surface pressure. Units and daily dates are checked before simulation.

02 · CROP

Crop water demand

ETc = Kc × ET₀

Crop coefficients vary through growth stages, with documented wind and humidity adjustment. Corn, soybean and sorghum parameters trace to FAO-56 tables.

Planting dates, fixed maximum root depths and stage lengths are scenario assumptions. They are not confirmed for every US climate, cultivar or farm.

03 · SOIL

A daily balance

ΔStorage = Rain − Runoff + Irrigation − Crop use − Drainage

USDA SSURGO supplies mapped soil water capacity and restrictions. USDA curve numbers estimate runoff. Rain-fed deficit and net irrigation requirement are calculated separately.

Net irrigation excludes conveyance and application losses. A millimeter of depth does not mean a farm should apply that amount today.

COVERAGE & RESOLUTION

Zoom reveals geography.
It doesn’t add scientific detail.

The geographic interface accepts locations within the 50 states, including Alaska and Hawaii. Weather is regional gridded data; radiation is coarser. Soil availability, missing records, frozen conditions and local crop suitability can limit a result.

Published map scenarios use a 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude meteorological grid, with coarser 1° radiation and a cell-center soil scenario. Transparent cells indicate missing, unsupported or uncomputed data. They are never silently interpolated.

NASA IMERG precipitation-rate imagery and SMAP assimilated soil-moisture imagery provide separately labelled context. Colors are not used to derive numerical irrigation requirements.
